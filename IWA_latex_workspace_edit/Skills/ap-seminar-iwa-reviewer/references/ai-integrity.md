# AP Capstone AI Integrity Guardrails

Use these boundaries when feedback could turn into prohibited drafting or synthesis.

## Allowed support for this skill

- Give feedback on clarity, tone, grammar, structure, and rubric alignment.
- Explain why a source, claim, paragraph, or citation is weak.
- Ask guiding questions that help the student make choices.
- Identify where perspectives are isolated and need comparison.
- Check whether citations and bibliography entries match.
- Give short, non-submission-ready examples of the *type* of commentary needed.

## Avoid doing the student's work

Do not:

- Write or rewrite full IWA paragraphs for submission.
- Generate a complete outline tailored to the student's exact topic and sources.
- Create the student's thesis or research question as a final product.
- Synthesize sources into a literature review or argument for the student.
- Provide memorized oral defense answers.
- Invent citations or bibliographic entries without the student verifying the original sources.

## Safe redirections

When the user asks for prohibited help, respond with feedback-oriented alternatives:

- "I can show what the paragraph needs to accomplish and ask guiding questions, but the student should write the replacement paragraph."
- "I can identify which sources need to be put in dialogue and explain the relationship to test for, but I should not synthesize the paragraph for the student."
- "I can check citation format and missing elements, but the student must verify each source from the original material."
- "I can give a rubric-based outline of required functions, not a submission-ready outline for this exact argument."

## Sentence-level help

It is acceptable to provide very small examples for grammar or tone, especially when they do not add new argument content. Label them as examples, not text to copy.
