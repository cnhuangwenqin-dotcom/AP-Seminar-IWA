# 2026 AP Seminar PT2 Stimulus Index

Use this only to help recognize current-year stimulus material and likely thematic connections. Do not assume the stimulus is correctly used just because it is cited; Row 1 requires authentic argumentative function.

## 2026 stimulus materials

1. **"Spaces of Consumption, Connection, and Community: Exploring the Role of the Coffee Shop in Urban Lives"** — Jennifer Ferreira, Carlos Ferreira, Elizabeth Bos; Geoforum; coffee shops, third places, urban sociality, community, consumption, isolation, public space.
2. **"Chapter 2: How Social Connection Impacts Individual Health and Well-Being"** — Office of the Surgeon General of the United States; loneliness, isolation, health, well-being, social connection, community.
3. **"How Roads Have Transformed the Natural World"** — Ben Goldfarb; Smithsonian Magazine; infrastructure, roads, ecology, habitat fragmentation, human-built systems, environmental transformation.
4. **"A Walk to Kobe"** — Haruki Murakami; Granta Magazine; walking, memory, place, disaster, human experience, movement through landscape.
5. **"35 Years After the Berlin Wall Opened, Fragments of East Germany's Border Remain"** — Associated Press; borders, memory, historical remnants, division, political geography, collective memory.
6. **"Dad, Ballpark Will Live On in Shared Memories"** — Ann Killion; SF Gate; sports place, family memory, shared memory, community, nostalgia, place attachment.
7. **"Sustainability Assessment of Low Earth Orbit (LEO) Satellite Broadband Megaconstellations"** — Osoro, Ogutu B., et al.; arXiv; satellite megaconstellations, sustainability, space infrastructure, technology, environment, orbital commons.

## Likely broad themes

Possible thematic bridges include:

- Connection, community, and isolation.
- Place, memory, and identity.
- Built environments and human relationships.
- Infrastructure shaping social or ecological life.
- Public/common spaces and access.
- Mobility, barriers, borders, and fragmentation.
- Sustainability of human systems, from roads to orbital infrastructure.
- Nostalgia, shared memory, and the persistence of physical remnants.

## Row 1 checking questions

Ask:

- Which two or more 2026 stimulus sources appear to have prompted the research question?
- Does the paper explicitly or implicitly derive its research question from a shared theme?
- Is at least one stimulus source integrated into the argument accurately?
- Would removing the stimulus source weaken the paper's context, claim, or reasoning?
- Is the stimulus being used for something only it can provide, or merely as a generic definition/source of facts?

## Coaching examples for stimulus use

Good functions:

- Using the coffee-shop article to frame a research question about whether urban third places can reduce social isolation.
- Using the Surgeon General chapter to establish why social connection has public-health stakes.
- Using the roads article to motivate a question about the unintended ecological consequences of infrastructure.
- Using the Berlin Wall article or ballpark article to frame how physical places preserve collective memory.
- Using the LEO megaconstellation article to frame a question about technology, access, and sustainability.

Weak functions:

- Mentioning a stimulus title once in the introduction and never connecting it to the argument.
- Using a stimulus only to define a common word.
- Citing a stimulus source for a generic fact that a more relevant external source would better support.
- Making a thematic leap so broad that almost any source could fit.
